Hiding

Cloak (maps / fd)

—›

Hide selected Xposed/LSPosed module APKs from every process's /proc/<pid>/maps and /proc/<pid>/fd via the kernel pathhide interface — applied live and re-applied on boot.

Hidden paths

—›

Makes a stock ROM file disappear — for paths that are themselves the tell, like install-recovery.sh. Saved to a list and re-applied every boot. Scan proposes paths that exist on this device and are worth hiding.

Foreign mounts

—›

Mounts another module laid over the ROM. Any app can read these in its own mount table, which is the whole thing a mountless setup avoids. Absorb re-serves what it safely can as injections and unmounts them; it runs at every boot.

Per-UID hiding

—›
Scan finds apps installed here worth hiding from and says why — nothing is hidden until you pick it. The globs are the part a scan cannot give you: they keep matching detectors installed later, or repackaged under a new name.
What hiding covers

Shows the listed app the stock filesystem — no injected files, no hidden paths. One entry covers the app in every user, work profile and clone. Use a package name: it survives a reinstall, a raw UID doesn't. Root and su are unaffected, and so is anything a system service reads on the app's behalf.

A glob covers a moving target: *.duckdetector, me.garfieldhan.* or *chunqiu* re-match on every apply, so a detector that reinstalls under a new package name stays hidden. Anchors go at the ends only, and a glob needs at least four literal characters.

Why this trades one tell for another

An isolated process's UID says nothing about which app started it, so hiding from one app means hiding from every isolated process. That closes the hole where a hidden app probes through a helper — but while it's on, an app that isn't hidden can spot the difference between its own view and its helper's. Both pools are covered by default.
